A year ago, I joined Stanford's Human-Centered AI Institute. Here are some reflections on what I've learned.

## The State of AI Safety

The field has matured significantly. We've moved from abstract philosophical debates to concrete technical challenges:

- **Alignment**: How do we ensure AI systems do what we intend?
- **Robustness**: How do we make systems that fail gracefully?
- **Transparency**: How do we understand what systems are doing?

## What's Working

### 1. Interpretability Research

Recent advances in mechanistic interpretability have been encouraging. We can now understand, at least partially, how certain neural networks arrive at their decisions.

### 2. Red-Teaming Approaches

Systematic adversarial testing has become standard practice. This shift from "ship fast" to "ship safe" is crucial.

### 3. Cross-Disciplinary Collaboration

The integration of perspectives from philosophy, law, and social science has enriched our technical work immeasurably.

## What Keeps Me Up at Night

Despite progress, significant challenges remain:

1. **Capability elicitation**: We don't fully understand what models can do
2. **Emergent behaviors**: Complex systems produce unexpected outputs
3. **Deployment pressures**: Commercial incentives sometimes override safety

## What Keeps Me Motivated

The researchers I work with give me hope. The AI safety community combines rigorous technical skills with genuine concern for humanity's future. That combination is rare and precious.
